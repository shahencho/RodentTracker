# No drop-in mouse model fits our video

Pretrained lab-mouse detectors and pose models do exist and can be downloaded today. **None of them is all three of: matched to our footage, CPU-light, and licensed for a product we sell.** The models whose training data looks most like our video (a small albino mouse on a white floor, top-down, monochrome) are the DeepLabCut SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse model, the Kumar Lab (JAX) 2019 single-mouse tracker, and DAMM (Detect-Any-Mouse-Model). Each has a catch. SuperAnimal's model card limits it to academic, non-commercial use ([HF](https://huggingface.co/mwmathis/DeepLabCutModelZoo-SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse)). DAMM is CC BY-NC-ND ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393305/)). Kumar's tracker needs TensorFlow 1.x and Python 2.7, and the licence of its weights is unstated ([GitHub](https://github.com/KumarLabJax/MouseTracking)). The genuinely YOLO-format, CPU-friendly mouse models are tiny community uploads with undocumented training data. Examples are KUNIN's YOLOv8n-pose, a white-rat YOLOv8s-pose and DeepRodent, and each is a long shot. Roboflow Universe models cannot be downloaded as weights at all. Nobody publishes accuracy on white-on-white footage or on mice under ~50 px, so a half-day bake-off on the YSMU video is the only way to know. Licensing is the deciding factor: the realistic route to a sellable tool is background subtraction for the centroid plus a small detector we train ourselves. Pretrained models serve as internal benchmarks and label generators, not as shipped components.

## Three domain-matched models, all with licence or tooling strings

**SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse (DeepLabCut) is the most mature zero-shot top-view mouse model.** It was trained on about 5,000 overhead images pooled from roughly 9–13 lab datasets ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.07436v4)). Those datasets explicitly include **elevated plus maze and open-field arenas and a "WhiteMice" set from the Golden lab** ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/mwmathis/DeepLabCutModelZoo-SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse)). Weights auto-download from Hugging Face. They include two Faster R-CNN detectors, ResNet50-FPN-v2 (173 MB) and a lighter **MobileNetV3-Large-FPN (76 MB)**, plus several pose heads: HRNet-w32/w48, ResNet-50/101 and **RTMPose-s (23 MB)**/m/x ([HF files](https://huggingface.co/mwmathis/DeepLabCutModelZoo-SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse/tree/main)). The output is a unified 26- or 27-keypoint top-view skeleton; the sources disagree on the count ([DLC Model Zoo](https://deeplabcut.github.io/DeepLabCut/docs/ModelZoo.html)). The keypoints should include nose, a mouse-centre point and tail base, but check that against the config after download. Zero-shot error on held-out open-field data was about **4.9 px RMSE for HRNet-w32** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2203.07436v4)).

Two built-in features matter for us. `video_adapt=True` self-trains on our own video, and `scale_list` compensates for animals far smaller than those in training ([DLC docs](https://deeplabcut.github.io/DeepLabCut/docs/ModelZoo.html)). The authors themselves warn of "**poor generalisation to diverse white-mouse populations**" and degraded accuracy under atypical lighting ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/mwmathis/DeepLabCutModelZoo-SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse)), which is exactly our case. The licence is "Modified MIT… academic, non-commercial purposes only". The DeepLabCut code is LGPL-3.0, and a commercial licence for the models has to be negotiated with EPFL-TTO ([DeepLabCut GitHub](https://github.com/DeepLabCut/DeepLabCut)). No CPU benchmark exists. We estimate MobileNet plus RTMPose-s will run at a few fps on 640x480, which is fine for offline analysis but not real-time.

**The Kumar Lab 2019 tracker is the closest published match to our footage, but it is the hardest to run.** Geuther et al. trained on top-down 480x480 monochrome frames, captured at 640x480 and 30 fps. The coat colours explicitly include **albino**, the arenas include a **white PVC floor**, and night recordings used 940 nm IR ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6440983/)). The paper's motivating result is that the classical tracker Ctrax broke down on albino mice and reflective surfaces while the network did not. The segmentation network reached a **~0.9 px centre error and mean IoU 0.828** ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6440983/)). Five pretrained models are offered, including AllCoatColor_RegularOFA and FullModel, via a JAX FTP tarball and Zenodo record 5806397 ([kumarlab.org](https://www.kumarlab.org/2019/02/12/pre-trained-single-mouse-tracking-neural-network-models/)). The output is an ellipse (centre, axes, angle), which suits centroid-plus-heading tracking.

The costs are significant. The code is MIT but pinned to **TensorFlow 1.0.1–1.4 and Python 2.7**, last pushed in 2019 ([GitHub](https://github.com/KumarLabJax/MouseTracking)). Running it on a Windows CPU means building a legacy environment or converting the graph to ONNX. The weights' licence, file format and FTP reachability were not verified. Kumar's later 12-keypoint HRNet pose model is PyTorch (115 MB), but it ships inside a Linux Singularity image and its data is released under a **non-commercial** licence ([Zenodo 6380163](https://zenodo.org/records/6380163)).

**DAMM is the most deliberately general mouse detector.** It is a Mask R-CNN trained on 2,200 images sampled from about 12,500 videos. Those videos cover top and side views, black, agouti and white coats, white and red light, and RGB and grayscale footage. Zero-shot results on unseen public setups were **95.9–99.5% mask AP75**, but only 58.1% on a complex low-light rig and 26.9% on a fisheye rat chamber ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393305/)). Twenty labelled frames lifted one hard setting from 56.4% to 84.7%. It runs at 11 FPS on a V100, which likely means about 1 FPS or less on a CPU (our inference). Weights are served from the [repo](https://github.com/backprop64/DAMM) under **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, so we cannot ship it or distribute derivatives.

## YOLO-format weights are small, cheap to test and poorly documented

The true "download a .pt and call `YOLO()`" options are few, and none documents a domain like ours. **KUNIN/mice-yolo-inf** holds a YOLOv8n-pose model (`kunin-mice-pose.v0.1.5n`, about 5.8 MB) plus OpenVINO fp32, fp16 and **int8 (about 3 MB)** exports, with a **CC BY 4.0** tag ([HF](https://huggingface.co/api/models/KUNIN/mice-yolo-inf)). A sibling repo documents it as a single-mouse model with **8 keypoints**: nose, two ears, body, two legs, tail base and tail tip ([HF](https://huggingface.co/KUNIN/single-mouse-webrtc-pose)). The training data, view and accuracy are all undocumented, and the only demo image is a cartoon mouse. It is the cheapest CPU test available and the only domain-plausible model with a commercially permissive tag. Even so, a one-line README tag from an unknown author is weak licence evidence.

**S4H0ST RatTracker** is a YOLOv8s-pose with 3 keypoints (snout, spine, tail), trained on **white rats in a white arena filmed overhead**. It reports mAP50 0.82 and a 92.1% detection rate on one test video, from a training set of only 248 original images ([GitHub](https://github.com/S4H0ST/Computer-Vision-Mice-Tracking)). The .pt is not in the repo. It ships inside a 350 MB PyInstaller release, `RatTracker_lab.zip`, from which it would have to be extracted ([release](https://github.com/S4H0ST/Computer-Vision-Mice-Tracking/releases/download/v1.0/RatTracker_lab.zip)). The licence is GPL-3.0. It is the only YOLO model found that was trained on white animals on white, but rats are much larger than our 30 px mouse.

**DeepRodent** is an Apache-2.0 Ultralytics .pt (6.8 MB) that claims detection, oriented-box and segmentation heads. It reports mAP50 96.2 on a private 30K-frame dataset ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/kaopanboonyuen/DeepRodent)). Its sample images show an oblique IR cage, not an open field, and its demo Space contains placeholder assets ([Space](https://huggingface.co/api/spaces/kaopanboonyuen/DeepRodent-Demo)), so treat the claims sceptically. **OpenFieldAI** bundles pretrained "OFAI 160/320/640" YOLO models for the open-field test inside a 2.1 GB GPLv3 Windows zip ([SourceForge](https://sourceforge.net/projects/openfieldai/)). Its training species, coat colour and YOLO version were not discoverable.

Several apparent leads turn out to be dead ends. **YOLO-MousePose** (IEEE TIM 2025) ships only a generic backbone and stock COCO weights, with no mouse weights and no licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/bujihao/YOLO-MousePose)). The **IVC-cage YOLOv8n showcase** is a README with images: no weights and no licence ([GitHub](https://github.com/StalmokasGrazvydas/ivc-cage-showcase)). It does document a useful fine-tuning recipe: grayscale, CLAHE, noise and blur augmentation, at about 7.5 FPS on CPU with ONNX Runtime. On **Roboflow Universe**, community projects such as "open field test" (246 images) and lab_mouse_detection (144 images) carry hosted models ([Universe](https://universe.roboflow.com/muza-2wj2q/open-field-test)). However, Universe weights cannot be downloaded as .pt files ([forum](https://discuss.roboflow.com/t/how-can-i-get-the-native-pt-file-of-the-https-universe-roboflow-com-cricket-qnb5l-basketball-xil7x-model/12294/3)). They run only through Roboflow's API or a key-gated local inference server ([docs](https://docs.roboflow.com/datasets/universe/universe/find-a-model-on-universe)). Their sample content and licences could not be verified because Universe blocked automated access.

The remaining pose model zoos do not fit:

- **SLEAP** ships no zero-shot mouse model. Its public mouse sets are a bottom-view C57 open field and a top-view white Swiss Webster home cage ([SLEAP datasets](https://legacy.sleap.ai/datasets.html)).
- **MARS** is built for a black resident plus white intruder pair in a home cage ([GitHub](https://github.com/neuroethology/MARS)).
- **AP-10K/MMPose** animal models are trained on side-view wildlife photos ([AP-10K](https://github.com/AlexTheBad/AP-10K)).

## Generic open-vocabulary models trade speed for flexibility

Stock COCO YOLO11 and YOLO-World text prompts found the mouse in 0–1 of 120 frames. That is consistent with evidence that open-vocabulary detectors degrade sharply off their natural-image domain ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.22164v1)). The one fast fix still untried is a **YOLOE visual prompt**. YOLOE accepts an example box drawn on a reference frame, so we can draw a box around our own mouse. The paper says visual prompts help with objects that text describes poorly ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.07465)), and the n/s sizes run on CPU at reduced resolution. The Ultralytics docs warn that zero-shot accuracy stays "well below a model trained on your classes" ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/yoloe/)). Ultralytics models are AGPL-3.0, with a paid Enterprise licence for closed products (not re-verified this session). YOLO-World in Ultralytics cannot take visual prompts at all.

The heavier models are labelling tools, not trackers:

- **SAM 2.1** can track from a single click with `SAM2VideoPredictor`. Ultralytics benchmarks SAM2-t at about 23 s per image on an M4 CPU, in what is probably segment-everything mode, against 24 ms for YOLO11n-seg ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/sam-2/)). SAM 2-based trackers also struggle with tiny objects and with objects that disappear ([FMOX review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/benchmarking-sam2-based-trackers-on-fmox)).
- **OWLv2** offers image-guided one-shot detection but upsamples to 960 px ([HF](https://huggingface.co/docs/transformers/model_doc/owlv2)). Grounding DINO and Florence-2 (MIT; [Roboflow](https://roboflow.com/model/florence-2)) cost seconds per frame on a CPU.
- **T-Rex2** is API-only ([GitHub](https://github.com/IDEA-Research/T-Rex)). SAM 3 is a 3.45 GB gated checkpoint ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/sam-3/)).

## Ranked shortlist and fallback plan

Apply the same preprocessing to every candidate: crop to the arena ROI, upscale the mouse region 2–3x so it reaches about 60–90 px, apply CLAHE, and replicate the grayscale channel to 3 channels. On a 30 px target this likely matters as much as the choice of model.

| Rank | Candidate | Test configuration | What it settles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SuperAnimal-TopViewMouse | Two configurations: RTMPose-s with the MobileNet detector, and HRNet-w32 with the ResNet50 detector. Use `video_adapt=True` and `scale_list` in both. If the detector misses, feed the pose head crops from our own background-subtraction box. | Whether learned keypoints (nose, tail base) are reachable at all. Evaluation only unless EPFL licenses it. |
| 2 | Kumar 2019 FullModel or AllCoatColor | Only if a TF1 environment or ONNX conversion takes under a day | Strongest domain match, and its licence may be the most negotiable |
| 3 | DAMM zero-shot | On a few hundred frames | Fitness as an offline pseudo-labeller for research-phase work |
| 4 | KUNIN int8 OpenVINO model | 10-minute test | Lottery ticket; the only permissive and CPU-ready option |
| 5 | YOLOE with a visual prompt | Prompt box taken from a YSMU frame | Generic-model check |
| 6 | Long shots | S4H0ST rat model (extracted .pt), DeepRodent, OpenFieldAI, Roboflow "try this model" widgets with 2–3 frames | Same question, low odds |

Score each candidate on the share of 120 sampled frames with a correct hit, with rearing and wall-hugging frames separated out.

If nothing works, which is the likeliest outcome for a commercial product, follow these steps:

1. **Track the centroid with a tuned classical pipeline.** Use a static-background model of the empty arena, contrast stretching and frame differencing, with a single-object Kalman filter. Kumar's paper shows the albino-on-white failure mode of naive thresholding ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6440983/)), so this needs careful tuning and a confidence signal.
2. **Generate training labels.** Pseudo-label a few hundred YSMU frames with SAM 2.1 click-tracking on short clips (Apache-2.0, not re-verified) or with the classical tracker itself. Then hand-correct rearing and wall frames. DAMM's 20-shot result (56.4% to 84.7% AP75) suggests a small in-domain set closes most of the gap ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11393305/)). The tooling for this distillation workflow already exists in Autodistill (Apache-2.0) ([GitHub](https://github.com/autodistill/autodistill)).
3. **Train our own small detector.** Train a nano detector, optionally with 3–8 keypoints, using the IVC showcase's augmentation recipe. Export it to ONNX; YOLO11n-class models run at about 24 ms per frame on a laptop CPU ([Ultralytics](https://docs.ultralytics.com/models/sam-2/)). If we train with Ultralytics, budget for the AGPL/Enterprise question or choose a permissively licensed trainer.

Two cautions apply. Labels produced by non-commercial models (SuperAnimal, DAMM) sit in a legal grey area for training a commercial model, so use them only for evaluation or get permission first. Also, the DAMM Hugging Face dataset declares no licence ([HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/gauravkaul/DAMM_mouse_detection)), and the Roboflow licences are unverified, so we should not assume either can be used commercially.

## Conclusion

The bottleneck here is licensing and domain, not the model architecture. The research community solved "track one mouse from above" years ago, but released its solutions under academic terms and tuned them for dark mice on contrasting floors. The permissively licensed weights that are easy to run on a CPU come with no documented provenance. The pretrained models are therefore most valuable to us as yardsticks: if SuperAnimal or the Kumar tracker succeeds on YSMU, that proves the footage is learnable and sets an accuracy target for our own model. The product moat is then a small, in-house-labelled white-on-white dataset, because nothing public covers that niche.

Prioritise one open question: does the Kumar 2019 model, the only one trained on albino mice on a white floor, transfer to a tilted 640x480 camera? If it does, and JAX will license the weights commercially, it could shortcut most of the fallback work.
